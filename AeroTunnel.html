About

A real-time lattice-Boltzmann wind tunnel by Sarujan Srikaran @SarujanSrikaran. Forces are measured from the simulation itself — sweep the angle slider to trace the lift curve live.

The physics: D2Q9 Lattice-Boltzmann with BGK collision plus Smagorinsky-style eddy-viscosity stabilisation (local eddy viscosity from the non-equilibrium stress tensor) — a turbulence-modelling idea borrowed from LES that keeps higher Reynolds numbers stable on a browser-scale grid; this is not a validation-grade LES setup. Boundaries: velocity inlet on the left, far-field top/bottom, and a simple open outlet approximation on the right (zero-gradient copy behind a viscous sponge layer that absorbs pressure waves before they can reflect). Aerodynamic forces come from momentum exchange on surface cells only, counting fluid-side links only — CL/CD are measured, not looked up. Smoke tracers are advected with 2nd-order Runge–Kutta so they hug streamlines instead of drifting through curvature. Limits of honesty: 2-D flow, low Mach, model-scale Reynolds numbers — absolute coefficients differ from full-scale flight; the trends (lift vs α, stall, shedding) are the real thing. Wind speed is capped at u₀ = 0.150 lattice units (Ma ≈ 0.26): the lattice-Boltzmann method is only valid well below its lattice sound speed cs = 1/√3, so arbitrarily high speeds are not physically or numerically possible in this scheme. The viscosity control enforces an adaptive lower bound (marked ν···*) at high speed and extreme α — the bound is a measured stability envelope of this exact solver, because at α ≈ 45° the model blocks over half the tunnel and local flow accelerates far beyond u₀.
